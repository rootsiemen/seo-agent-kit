# Brand Mentions & AI Citations

AI answers are assembled from what the web says about you — and most of it isn't your website. If your brand exists only on its own domain, models have nothing third-party to cite, and they cite whoever else shows up. This skill builds that footprint deliberately.

## Step 1 — Audit the baseline (expect zero)

Search the open web for your brand name and domain, exactly as an AI would encounter them:
- `"BrandName"` alone, `"branddomain.com"` alone, and brand + niche ("BrandName reviews").
- Record: how many third-party mentions exist at all, and — critically — **name collisions**. (Real case: "Frinjo" collides with FriendJo/FriendMe apps; the attorney directory's domain is drowned out by a similarly-named `.com`.) A verified baseline of "≈zero third-party footprint" is the normal starting point, not an embarrassment.

## Step 2 — The naming rule

**Always write "Name (domain)" together** — in profiles, posts, author bios, video descriptions, everywhere: `USA Attorneys (usaattorneys.company)`. This is how you teach both humans and models that the name and the domain are the same entity, and it disambiguates you from collisions.

## Step 3 — Tier-1 profiles (do these first)

One afternoon, in this order. Keep name, description, logo, and links identical across all of them.

1. **LinkedIn company page** — the profile AI models weight heavily for "is this a real business".
2. **Crunchbase** — free basic profile; entity data feeds multiple AI pipelines.
3. **Medium publication/account** — 1–2 genuinely useful articles in your niche (not ads), each linking the brand naturally.
4. **Quora** — answer real questions in your niche with substance; brand in the credential line, link only where it truly helps.
5. **YouTube channel description + every video description** — if you publish video at all, this is free, indexed brand text.

### Profile copy structure (fill once, reuse everywhere)

```
[Name] ([domain]) is a [one-line what-it-is] for [audience].
It provides [2–3 concrete things], covering [scope/coverage].
[One verifiable fact: listing count, coverage, founding context.]
Website: https://[domain]
```

## Step 4 — Tier-2 (niche-specific)

Wherever your niche verifies credibility: for attorneys — Avvo/Justia/FindLaw-style directories; for local services — Yelp, Bing Places, industry associations; for products — Product Hunt, AlternativeTo, app directories. Ten accurate niche listings beat a hundred generic ones.

## Step 5 — Earn mentions, don't plant spam

- Answer questions (Quora/Reddit/forums) with the data your site uniquely has — directories and original guides are quotable assets.
- Publish the artifact others cite: benchmark posts, fee tables, survey summaries (see the [content-clusters skill](../content-clusters/SKILL.md)).
- Re-check the baseline monthly: new third-party mentions, new collisions, whether "Name (domain)" is being picked up verbatim.

## What this is not

Not link buying, not fake reviews, not comment spam. The footprint has to survive a human reading it — because increasingly, a human (or a model judging like one) does.
