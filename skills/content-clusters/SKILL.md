# Content Cluster Validation

Don't guess topics. Validate a cluster from evidence before writing — this is the exact method used on 2026-10-24 to green-light a lawyer-cost cluster for a US attorney directory, using nothing but the site's own data and public SERPs.

## Step 1 — Mine your own Search Console first

Export GSC Performance → Queries (3 months). The gold is not your top queries — it's the ones with **impressions at positions ~50–90**. Impressions there mean Google already associates your domain with the topic; you're not starting from zero, you're under-built. Group them into themes (on the attorney site: "business lawyer cost" ≈40 impressions, "divorce lawyer cost" ≈24, most at positions 78–91).

Also note the near-wins: any query already at positions ~8–25 is one good page away from traffic. Write those down separately.

## Step 2 — Confirm humans actually ask this

Search Reddit, Quora, and niche forums (for legal: Avvo Q&A, Lawyers.com Ask-a-Lawyer) for the same questions in natural language. You're checking that the demand is a real recurring human problem, not a keyword-tool artifact. Practitioners publishing their own fee schedules/FAQ pages for the topic is another strong demand signal.

## Step 3 — Read the SERP like an investor

For each target query, look at who ranks top 10:
- All giants (Forbes, Avvo, Wikipedia)? The cluster is a long war — deprioritize unless you have a local/angle wedge.
- **Small specialists, niche blogs, content farms ranking?** That's an open door. Note their domain age/authority signals, content length, and freshness.
- **Stale incumbents are the best targets.** A "#1 page" whose content is years out of date (one DUI-cost incumbent's page metadata showed it effectively untouched for ~4 years) can be beaten by a current, better-sourced page.

## Step 4 — Decide the build order

1. **Expand existing ranking pages first.** If a page already holds position ~11 or ~23, improving it protects an asset a new URL would throw away. Refresh the date only when the content genuinely changed.
2. **Flagships next** — the largest validated clusters, written to be the best page on the SERP: sourced data tables (real fee ranges with sources, e.g. published rate surveys), state/segment breakdowns, "what the price excludes" lists, FAQs.
3. **Spin-offs last** — state or sub-topic variants of a winning flagship (e.g. Florida + Texas divorce-cost tables inside, then separate pages if data supports them).

## Step 5 — The quality bar for every guide

- Real numbers with real sources (published surveys, practitioner schedules) — never invented statistics.
- Answer the money question in the first screenful; depth after.
- Tables beat paragraphs for cost content. Every claim a competitor makes vaguely, make specifically.

## Validate or kill

Green-light a cluster only if all three hold: (a) your own GSC shows latent demand, (b) humans ask the question in public forums, (c) beatable small/stale sites hold the SERP. Otherwise write the finding down and move on — a killed cluster is a successful validation too.
